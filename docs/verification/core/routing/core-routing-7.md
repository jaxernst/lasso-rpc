# core-routing-7

- `core-routing-7` A read that needs the current head prefers a route its head round measured at the current head over a route the round measured behind, in every strategy order, and keeps that preference while client head reads refresh the other routes' observations. Historical reads, hash lookups by block, tags such as `finalized`, and writes keep strategy order, so a slightly lagging provider still serves the blocks it has. A deferred route stays available when the current route fails.

[Area and shared preconditions](README.md).

Guard: `harness/test/head_freshness_test.exs` uses real HTTP RPC and owned fixture upstreams. Priority puts the lagging upstream first, so any request it loses is a head preference, not a strategy choice.

## Driving it through surfaces

Configure an owned file profile with three fixture upstreams in priority order, background observations on, `monitoring.http_heads_interval_ms: 600000` and `selection.max_lag_blocks: 10`, which still loads and has no effect. The primary answers `eth_blockNumber` with 9,950 and both alternates with 10,000. The head round that polls all three when the profile opens is the head evidence, and its long interval keeps later rounds out of the counted sends.

Require an alternate to serve `latest` and omitted-selector state reads (`eth_getBalance`, `eth_call`, `eth_estimateGas`, `eth_getTransactionCount`), `eth_blockNumber`, `eth_getTransactionReceipt`, and `eth_getLogs` ranges whose upper bound is `latest`, omitted or above the primary's head. Require the primary to serve numeric reads at 9,000, `eth_call` at `finalized`, `eth_getBlockByHash`, a range ending at 9,000, and `eth_sendRawTransaction`. Then make both alternates return 503 for a `latest` read and require the primary's answer after one send to each upstream. Finally, with a 20 ms `block_time_ms` and the primary 50 blocks behind, pin `eth_blockNumber` reads to an alternate for a second and require every `latest` read in that time to avoid the primary: the round's measured lag holds while the primary's own observation ages. Last, with only the primary and one alternate, the primary 50 blocks behind and a 1 s head interval, require `latest` reads to avoid the primary, then have the primary answer `eth_blockNumber` with 10,000 and require a later round to return `latest` reads to it: two routes are judged by their gap alone.

Seven more cases cover which rounds keep and decide a measured lag. With the primary deferred, open a second profile on three other fixture upstreams with a 1 s head interval and require the first profile's `latest` reads to keep avoiding the primary after those upstreams answer five rounds, while the first profile's upstreams are polled only once. With `probe_interval_ms: 1000` and `http_heads_interval_ms: 10000`, require every `latest` read for four seconds after the deferral to avoid the primary, before any second poll. Open a profile with background observations off on the same three upstreams as a profile that defers the primary, and require the heads-off profile's `latest` read to reach the primary. With a 1 s head interval, hold both alternates' `eth_blockNumber` answers until the primary's second poll shows the next tick has opened, then release them and require the first round to defer the primary. With the first profile on just the primary and one alternate, open two more profiles in turn, each pairing one of them with a fresh upstream and deferring its own lagging route, and require the first profile's `latest` reads to keep avoiding the primary. Feed the primary `newHeads` at its lagging height, defer it, hold the alternate's `eth_blockNumber` answers, and for two seconds step the primary's head forward and back one block, requiring every `latest` read to avoid it. With the primary and a witness at 10,000 and an outlier at 1,000,000, switch the witness to `newHeads` after the first round and require every `latest` read for 1.5 seconds to reach the primary.

- **core-routing-7.** Require the upstream named above for each request, counted from fixture sends between cursors.

The oracle is which fixture received each request. This establishes ordering by observed head, not that the preferred upstream's answer is canonical.
